Why I do this

I know these meetings from the inside.

For nine years, I helped lead ARD meetings as a school administrator. I know how school teams prepare and what goes into their decisions. I also saw how hard the process can be to follow from the parent’s side: unfamiliar terms, long documents, and a lot to take in at once.

I started Intuitive Education Pathways to help with that. I believe the best outcomes happen when parents and schools work together, and that starts with you feeling prepared and heard. You know your child best, and I can help you make sense of the rest.

  • One person, start to finish. You’ll work with me directly, from the first call through the follow-up.
  • Plain English. I’ll translate the acronyms and paperwork, so you always know what you’re agreeing to.
  • Confidence that lasts. You’ll understand the process well enough to handle future meetings, with or without me.

How it works

Support at every stage of the ARD process.

  1. 1

    Review your child’s plan

    We go through the current IEP, evaluations, and progress reports together. I explain what each part means, what’s working, and where there may be gaps.

  2. 2

    Prepare for the meeting

    We set priorities, write down your questions, and decide what to ask for. You’ll walk in with a plan and a clear sense of what to expect.

  3. 3

    Follow up afterward

    We review what was agreed, see how the plan is working day to day, and look ahead to what comes next. Your child’s needs will change as they grow, and I’ll be there for each step.

Common questions

What is an ARD meeting?+

In Texas, the ARD (Admission, Review, and Dismissal) committee is the team that writes and updates your child’s IEP. You are a member of that committee, along with teachers and school staff. It meets at least once a year, and you can request a meeting at any time.

Do I need an advocate if the school is already helping?+

Many schools work hard for their students. My role is to make sure you understand the process and your options, so you can take a full and confident part in every decision. That usually makes meetings more productive for everyone.

Are you an attorney?+

No. I’m a special education advocate, not a lawyer, so I don’t give legal advice. If your situation calls for legal help, I’ll point you toward resources that can provide it.
